How this was calculated

    What a shift differential is

    A shift differential is extra pay per hour for working undesirable hours - nights, evenings, weekends or holidays. It is set by your employer, union contract or policy, so check yours. The window and amount are therefore inputs here, pre-filled with an example (10:00 pm to 6:00 am), not rules.

    The calculator counts only the minutes of your shift that overlap the window, even when the window or the shift crosses midnight. Hours outside the window earn the base rate. The unpaid break is taken from the hours outside the window first, which is the assumption most favorable to the employee; if your policy treats the break differently the number will vary slightly.

    Worked example

    $20 base, $2 flat differential, 6:00 pm to 3:00 am, 30-minute break

    • Clock to clock: 9:00; minus the 30-minute break = 8:30 paid (8.5 hours).
    • Window 10:00 pm to 6:00 am overlaps the shift from 10 pm to 3 am = 5 hours.
    • Base pay: 8.5 × $20 = $170.00. Differential: 5 × $2 = $10.00. One shift = $180.00, an effective $21.18 per hour.
    • Five shifts: 42.5 hours. Straight-time pay = $900.00. The differential is part of the regular rate, so the weighted regular rate is $900 ÷ 42.5 = $21.18 and the overtime premium is 2.5 hours × 0.5 × $21.18 = $26.47. Weekly total = $926.47.

    Percent vs flat differentials

    A 10% differential on a $20 base is $2.00 an hour, the same as a flat $2. They diverge when your base rate changes: after a raise to $25 the percent version becomes $2.50 while the flat version stays $2. The calculator accepts either.

    For overtime, the Department of Labor says the regular rate includes all remuneration for employment except the payments the law specifically lets employers exclude; shift differentials are not among the exclusions in the DOL's list. That is why the weekly figure above uses a blended regular rate. Your employer's payroll should do the same.

    Common ways differentials are structured

    • Flat amount per hour for hours in a window (the example above): $2.00 for each hour between 10 pm and 6 am.
    • Percentage of base pay: 10% on evenings, 15% on nights.
    • Whole-shift differential: if most of the shift falls in the window the whole shift gets the premium. This calculator counts only the hours inside the window; for a whole-shift rule, set the window to cover the whole shift.
    • Weekend or holiday premium: handled the same way by changing the window.

    Because these vary by employer and contract, read your offer letter or policy and enter the numbers it states.

    Estimating the yearly value

    A $2.00 night differential

    Five shifts a week with 5 differential hours each is 25 hours × $2.00 = $50.00 a week, or $2,600 over 52 weeks (before any overtime effect). A 10% differential on a $20 base rate is the same $2.00, but it grows with raises.

    Checking your pay stub for differentials

    A correct stub shows the differential either as a separate earnings line (“night differential”, hours and rate) or as a blended rate. Compare the hours on that line with the hours your shift spent in the window. If you worked 9:00 pm to 5:30 am with a 10:00 pm start for the differential, the shift has 7.5 hours in the window (before any break), not the full 8.5 hours.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is a shift differential?

    Extra pay added to your hourly rate for working less desirable hours, such as nights or weekends.

    Is night shift pay required by law?

    Night-shift premiums are normally set by an employer policy or a union contract. Check your offer letter, contract and state labor department for anything required where you work.

    Does shift differential count toward overtime?

    The DOL says the regular rate includes all remuneration for employment except specific exclusions, and shift differentials are not on that exclusion list, so overtime generally is based on a rate that includes them.

    Can the differential window cross midnight?

    Yes. A window like 10:00 pm to 6:00 am is handled correctly.

    How do I calculate a percentage differential?

    Multiply the base rate by the percentage. 10% of $20 is $2.00.
