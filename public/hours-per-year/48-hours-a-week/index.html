48 Hours a Week: Hours Per Year, Month and Pay Period

Updated October 22, 2026 · Quick-answer page · How we write

How many hours 48 a week adds up to over a month and a year, what it pays at common hourly rates and when overtime starts.

The answer

2,496 hours a year
48 hours × 52 weeks = 2,496. That is 208 hours a month on average and 96 hours every two weeks.

Forty-eight hours is six eight-hour days, or four twelve-hour shifts. This schedule is 120% of a 40-hour week and counts as above full-time work.

Spread over a 5-day week that is 9.6 hours a day; over a 4-day week 12 hours a day; over 3 days 16 hours a day.

Worked example

48 hours a week at $20 an hour

Weekly gross = 40 h × $20 + 8 h × $30 = $1,040.00. Annual = $1,040.00 × 52 = $54,080.00. Two unpaid weeks would remove $2,080.00, leaving $52,000.00.

Overtime

Over 40 hours a week, federal overtime applies to non-exempt employees: 40 regular hours plus 8 overtime hours each week. At $20 an hour that is $1,040.00 a week instead of $960.00 at straight time, a premium of $80.00 a week or $4,160.00 over 52 weeks. See overtime rules and the calculator for state differences.

Frequently asked questions

How many hours a year is 48 hours a week?

48 hours a week × 52 weeks = 2496 hours a year, before subtracting any unpaid time off.

How many hours a month is 48 hours a week?

About 208 hours: 48 × 52 ÷ 12.

What is 48 hours a week per day?

9.6 hours a day over five days, or 12 hours over four days.

Is 48 hours a week full time?

There is no single federal definition in the sources we cite; employers define it for benefits. A 40-hour week is the standard comparison and 48 hours is 120% of that.

Do I get overtime at 48 hours a week?

Under the federal rule, non-exempt employees receive overtime for hours over 40 in a workweek. At 48 hours that is 8 hours of overtime per week.
