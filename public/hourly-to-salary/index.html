How this was calculated

    The formula

    annual pay = hourly rate × hours per week × paid weeks per year

    A full-time year is usually counted as 40 × 52 = 2,080 hours. That is the figure behind most “hourly to salary” rules of thumb: double the hourly rate and add three zeros gets you within a few percent ($20/hour is about $41,600). Everything is gross pay, before taxes and deductions.

    Monthly pay is annual ÷ 12, bi-weekly is annual ÷ 26 and semi-monthly is annual ÷ 24. Weekly and daily are shown for a normal 5-day week.

    Worked examples

    $20 an hour, full time

    $20 × 40 × 52 = $41,600. Monthly: $41,600 ÷ 12 = $3,466.67. Bi-weekly check: $41,600 ÷ 26 = $1,600.00. Semi-monthly check: $41,600 ÷ 24 = $1,733.33.

    $20 an hour with two unpaid weeks off

    Paid weeks = 52 − 2 = 50. $20 × 40 × 50 = $40,000, which is $1,600 less than the full-year figure. Enter 2 in the unpaid weeks box to check your own year.

    $18.50 an hour, 32 hours a week

    $18.50 × 32 × 52 = $30,784. Part-time hours cut annual income proportionally, but benefits or eligibility rules can also depend on hours.

    Overtime is not included

    The calculator assumes straight time. A worker at $20 an hour regularly working 45 hours earns about 5 × $10 extra each week in overtime premium (5 hours at the extra half rate), or $2,600 a year more, if they are non-exempt under the federal rule. Use the overtime calculator for that.

    Hourly versus salary: what changes

    An hourly worker is paid for hours worked, and non-exempt hourly employees receive overtime pay over 40 hours in a workweek. A salaried employee is paid a fixed amount per pay period; whether overtime is owed depends on whether the job is exempt, not on the pay method. Our exempt vs non-exempt guide explains the difference. To go the other way, use the salary to hourly calculator.

    Beyond the base salary

    An hourly wage is only part of what a job pays. When you compare an hourly job with a salaried one, line up the extras: employer health insurance contributions, retirement matching, paid holidays and vacation, bonuses and overtime. Paid time off matters especially for the calculation above: if you are paid for vacation and holidays, use 52 weeks; if you work seasonally or are paid only for hours on the clock, enter your unpaid weeks. A person earning $22 an hour who is paid for 52 weeks makes $45,760, but the same person on a 10-month school-year schedule with 10 paid weeks fewer makes $22 × 40 × 42 = $36,960 unless the employer spreads pay over 12 months.

    A step-by-step conversion you can do on paper

    1. Write the hourly rate: say $23.50.
    2. Multiply by the hours you work in a week, say 38: $23.50 × 38 = $893.00.
    3. Multiply by the paid weeks, 52: $893.00 × 52 = $46,436.00.
    4. Divide by 12 for a monthly figure ($3,869.67), by 26 for a bi-weekly check ($1,786.00) or by 24 for a semi-monthly check ($1,934.83).

    Keep the unrounded numbers until the last step; rounding the weekly figure to the nearest dollar before multiplying by 52 can shift the result by tens of dollars.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do you convert hourly pay to an annual salary?

    Multiply the hourly rate by hours per week and by paid weeks per year. At 40 hours and 52 weeks, $20 an hour is $41,600.

    How many working hours are in a year?

    2,080 for a standard full-time year (40 hours x 52 weeks), before subtracting unpaid time off.

    Is $25 an hour a good salary?

    $25 an hour at 40 hours a week is $52,000 a year, or $2,000 per bi-weekly check, before taxes. Whether that is good depends on where you live and your costs.

    Does this include taxes?

    No. These are gross figures. Use the paycheck estimator to estimate take-home pay.

    Should I use 52 weeks or fewer?

    Use 52 if you are paid every week of the year, including paid vacation and holidays. Use fewer if you have unpaid time off, such as school-year or seasonal work.
