    Why this site is different

    Most time card calculators give a number and nothing else. Here you can see how the number was reached: each calculator lists the steps (minutes between times, unpaid breaks, regular versus overtime hours, pay) so you can check it against a pay stub, learn the method, or spot a mistake.

    • Real-life inputs. Overnight shifts, date-crossing spans, unpaid meals, bi-weekly and semi-monthly pay periods, workweek start days, bonuses in the regular rate, state overtime presets.
    • Worked examples and long explanations under every tool, written in plain English.
    • Sources, not guesses. Overtime, break and tax rules link to the U.S. Department of Labor, the IRS or the state agency, with the date we last checked. See our editorial policy.
    • Printable. Every result can be printed or saved as a PDF without menus or ads.
    • Private. Calculations happen on your device.

    How to get an accurate result

    1. Use the actual times you worked, not the scheduled ones.
    2. Enter only unpaid meal breaks; paid rest breaks stay inside your hours.
    3. Choose the overtime rule for the state where you work and the workweek start day your employer uses.
    4. Compare the answer with your pay stub. If they differ, read time card mistakes and how to fix them.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do I calculate my work hours?

    Subtract your clock-in time from your clock-out time and subtract any unpaid break. Our time card calculator does this for every day in a week, bi-weekly or semi-monthly period, and shows each step.

    Is my data private?

    Yes. Every calculator runs in your browser. The times and rates you enter are not sent to any server. A time card you fill in is saved only in your own browser's local storage, and you can clear it with the Reset button.

    Are these calculators free?

    Yes, with no sign-up. The site is built to be supported by advertising later, but the tools will stay free.

    Where do your overtime and break rules come from?

    From the U.S. Department of Labor, the IRS and state labor departments. Each page that states a rule links to its source and shows the date we last checked it. Where we could not confirm a rule from an official source, we left it out.

    Can I use this to run payroll?

    No. These are calculators for estimates and for checking your own numbers. Payroll has legal requirements this site does not cover. See the disclaimer.
