Updated October 22, 2026 · Index · How we write

Thirty-five common start and finish times, each answered with paid hours, weekly totals, pay and overtime.

Each page below answers it for one shift and goes further: clock-to-clock hours, paid hours after lunch, weekly and bi-weekly totals, pay at several hourly rates, overtime scenarios and a break table. How to use these pages

  • Clock-to-clock hours are simply end minus start. Paid hours subtract an unpaid meal.
  • Weekly totals assume five identical shifts. Bi-weekly totals are two workweeks, with overtime figured separately in each.
  • Overtime figures use the federal weekly rule (over 40 hours in the workweek), as described by the U.S. Department of Labor. State rules may differ; see the overtime rules guide.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most common shift length?

Eight paid hours is the familiar full-time day: nine hours clock to clock with a one-hour unpaid lunch, or eight and a half hours with a 30-minute lunch.

How do I convert a shift to hours?

Subtract the start time from the end time (add 12 hours to PM times), then subtract any unpaid meal.

What if my shift crosses midnight?

Use the hours calculator, which handles overnight shifts.

Which overtime rule do these pages use?

The federal weekly rule: hours over 40 in a workweek are paid at 1.5 times the regular rate, as described by the U.S. Department of Labor.
